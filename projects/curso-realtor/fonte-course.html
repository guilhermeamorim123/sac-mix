Module 01

Your Voice File

20 minutes · do this first

Every agent who tries AI and quits does so for the same reason: it writes like a brochure. This module fixes that permanently, and everything afterwards depends on it.

Why generic output happens

An AI with no information about you writes the average of every real estate listing ever published. That average is "Welcome to this stunning residence" — which is exactly what your competitors' listings say, because they are also using the average.

The fix is not a better prompt. It is giving the model a sample of you.

Build the file

Open a blank document. Paste in:

  • Three listings you wrote yourself and were happy with — before you ever used AI
  • Two client emails you sent that got a good reply
  • Your market: city, typical price band, who your buyers usually are
  • Five words you never use — most agents list "stunning", "nestled", "boasts", "dream home", "must see"

Save it. You will paste this at the top of a conversation once, and everything in that conversation inherits it.

Prompt 1 · Voice extraction

Below are samples of my own writing as a real estate agent. Read them and describe my voice in a short profile I can reuse: sentence length, how formal I am, whether I lead with the property or the buyer, words and phrases I actually use, and words I clearly avoid.

Do not flatter the writing. If it is plain, say plain. I want an accurate profile, not a compliment.

[paste your samples]

Keep what it gives you. That profile is now the first thing you paste into every workflow in this course.

The five-word rule

The single highest-return line in your voice file is the list of words you never use. Banning "stunning", "nestled" and "boasts" removes most of what makes AI writing detectable at a glance.

Module 02

Listing Descriptions

15 minutes to set up · 90 seconds per listing after

The task you do most and enjoy least. Set this up once and a description takes the time it takes to paste the facts.

Facts in, description out

The mistake is asking for a description without giving anything specific to work with. The model then invents adjectives to fill the gap. Give it facts and it has no reason to invent.

Prompt 2 · The listing description

[paste your voice profile]

Write a listing description using only the facts below. Rules:

- Open with the single most unusual thing about this property, not with a greeting
- No adjective that is not supported by a fact I gave you
- 140 to 170 words
- Last line is a concrete next step, not "schedule your showing today"
- If a fact is missing that you need, ask me instead of guessing

FACTS:
[beds / baths / sqft / year / lot]
[three things a buyer would notice walking in]
[one honest drawback]
[who this home actually suits]

Why you include the drawback

Two reasons, and the second matters more. First, a description that admits something reads as honest and holds attention longer. Second, it forces the model to write around a real constraint, which kills the generic template.

You are not publishing the drawback. You are giving the writer something true to work with, and you cut that line before posting if you want.

Fair housing

Never describe the people a neighbourhood suits — describe the property. "Great for families", "perfect for young professionals" and anything about schools, churches or demographics can breach fair housing rules. This is not an AI problem; it is the same rule as always. But AI will produce those phrases happily if you let it, so add them to your never-use list.

Prompt 4 · Personalising one lead

Here is my sequence and here is what I know about this lead. Fill every bracket using only what I have told you. If you cannot fill a bracket from what I gave you, delete that sentence rather than inventing a detail.

LEAD: [what they enquired about, budget, timeline, anything they said]

The instruction that matters most

"Delete rather than invent" is the line that makes this safe to send. Without it the model will confidently write that the lead mentioned a growing family when they never did — and you will not notice until they do.

Module 04

Social Posts from One Listing

20 minutes

You already did the work of understanding the property. This turns that one effort into twelve posts across four platforms.

Prompt 5 · The multiplier

[paste your voice profile]

From the listing below, write twelve social posts:

- 3 for Instagram (caption plus 5 hashtags, first line must work as a hook alone)
- 3 for Facebook (longer, conversational, one question at the end)
- 3 for LinkedIn (angle: market insight, property as the example)
- 3 short scripts for a 20-second video, written to be spoken aloud

Each of the twelve must lead with a different angle: the price, the feature, the location, the buyer's problem it solves. Do not repeat the opening structure.

LISTING: [paste your description from Module 2]

The angle rule

Twelve posts about the same house become annoying if they say the same thing twelve times. Forcing a different opening angle each time is what makes a single property carry two weeks of content.

Module 05

The Neighbourhood Guide

45 minutes · the highest-value hour in this course

A guide to one neighbourhood is the asset that makes you the obvious local agent. It works as a lead magnet, an email attachment and a listing appointment leave-behind.

Prompt 6 · The outline

I am building a buyer's guide to [NEIGHBOURHOOD, CITY]. Outline it in sections. For each section, tell me exactly what local facts you need from me — I will supply them, you will not guess.

Assume the reader is deciding between this neighbourhood and two others, and wants the honest trade-offs, not a brochure.

Do not let it invent local facts

This is the module where AI will most confidently make things up: schools that do not exist, a farmers market on the wrong day, a commute time it guessed. Every local fact comes from you. The model writes; it does not research. Publishing an invented local detail is how a local expert stops being believed.

Prompt 7 · The draft

[paste your voice profile]

Write the guide from the outline and the facts below. Rules: every factual claim traces to a fact I gave you. Where a trade-off exists, name it plainly. Include one section on who this neighbourhood does not suit.

FACTS: [paste yours]

Module 06

Objection Scripts

25 minutes

Not generic objection handling. Your actual objections, in your words, ready to paste into a reply.

Start from your inbox

Open your sent folder and find the last ten times a client pushed back. Those are your objections. Generic lists from a sales book are not.

Prompt 8 · Your objections

[paste your voice profile]

Below are real objections I have received. For each, write a reply that:

- Acknowledges the concern as reasonable, in one line, without flattery
- Gives one concrete piece of information that addresses it
- Ends by returning the decision to them, not by pushing
- Is under 90 words and sounds like an email, not a script

Do not write anything that promises an outcome I cannot control — no predictions about prices, rates or how fast something will sell.

OBJECTIONS: [paste yours]

The line that keeps you out of trouble

"Do not promise an outcome I cannot control" belongs in every prompt where the output goes to a client. Left out, the model will happily write that rates are about to drop or the home will sell in two weeks. In writing, from you, that is a problem.

Module 07

Listing Presentation Prep

20 minutes per appointment

Walking in knowing the street, the comparable sales and the likely objection is the difference between presenting and winning.

Prompt 9 · The prep brief

I have a listing appointment. Below is what I know about the property, the seller and three comparable sales. Give me:

1. The three questions this seller is most likely to ask, based on what I told you
2. The strongest argument for my pricing, in plain language
3. The weakest point in my position, stated honestly, and how I would address it
4. One question I should ask them that most agents will not

Do not invent market data. Use only the comparables I gave you.

DETAILS: [paste yours]

Point 3 is the one to read twice. The model is better at finding the hole in your own position than you are, because it is not attached to it.

Module 08

Set It Up Once: Projects & Skills

30 minutes · then you never paste your voice profile again

Everything so far assumed you paste your voice profile at the top of each conversation. This module removes that step permanently. It is the difference between a trick you use sometimes and a system you actually keep.

Projects: context that stays

A Project in Claude is a workspace with a memory. Anything you put in its knowledge stays available to every conversation inside it — you upload once, not every time.

Create one Project called Listings. Into its knowledge, put:

  • Your voice profile from Module 1
  • Your never-use word list
  • Three listings you were happy with
  • Your market basics: city, price bands, typical buyer

Now every chat inside that Project already knows how you write. The prompts in Modules 2 through 7 get shorter — you delete the "paste your voice profile" line, because it is already there.

One Project per job, not one for everything

Make a second Project called Farming for neighbourhood guides and market updates. Different job, different reference material. A single Project holding everything gets vague, and vague context produces vague writing.

Skills: rules that apply themselves

A Skill is a set of instructions Claude picks up when the task matches. Where a Project holds reference, a Skill holds procedure — the format, the constraints, the things that must never happen.

The single highest-value Skill for an agent is one that carries your listing rules, because it makes the rules automatic instead of remembered.

Prompt 42 · Have Claude write the Skill for you

I want a Skill that handles writing listing descriptions in my voice. Write the instructions for it. It must always:

- Open with the most unusual thing about the property, never a greeting
- Use no adjective unsupported by a fact I supplied
- Stay between 140 and 170 words
- End on a concrete next step
- Never describe the people a neighbourhood suits — fair housing
- Never use: stunning, nestled, boasts, dream home, must see
- Ask me for a missing fact instead of inventing one

Write it as clear instructions, not as a prompt I paste. Keep it short enough that I can read and correct it.

Read what it produces before you save it. A Skill you have not read is a rule you did not agree to — and it will apply to everything.

Artifacts: for anything longer than an email

When you ask for the neighbourhood guide from Module 5, Claude builds it as a document beside the conversation instead of burying it in chat. You edit by asking for changes, and the document updates in place. Use it for guides, presentations and anything you will hand to a client.

When to start a fresh conversation

Long conversations drift — Claude starts weighting things you said forty messages ago about a different property. One conversation per listing. When the listing is done, start a new chat. The Project keeps the context that matters; the chat should not.

Module 09

The First Five Minutes

20 minutes

No prompt in this course matters as much as how fast you answer. This module is about the part AI does not do for you — and how to make it fast enough that AI has something to work with.

Speed beats polish, and it is not close

A lead who fills a form is comparing you against everyone else they contacted that hour. The agent who replies first gets the conversation, and the conversation is what you are actually competing for. A perfect email sent tomorrow loses to a plain one sent in five minutes.

This is why the follow-up engine in Module 3 exists as pre-written drafts. Not so you can send them blind — so that at 21:40 you are editing two lines instead of writing from nothing.

The first reply: one question, no pitch

The instinct is to prove value immediately — attach listings, explain your process, describe the market. It reads as a brochure and it ends the exchange.

A first reply does three things and stops: confirms you are a person, asks one easy question, and says what happens next.

Prompt 43 · The first reply

[Project: Listings]

Write a first reply to a new lead. Three lines maximum. Confirm I got their enquiry, ask exactly one question that is easy to answer in a sentence, and say what I will do next. No attachments, no market commentary, no pitch. It should read like a person typing quickly, not like a template.

THEY ASKED ABOUT: [paste the enquiry]

Log what they said, in their words

After every conversation, write one line of what they actually said — their phrasing, not your summary. "Wants somewhere the dog can run" is usable later. "Interested in outdoor space" is not.

Those exact words become the personalisation in Module 3 and the hook in every message afterwards. This is the whole reason your follow-ups will sound different from everyone else's.

Prompt 44 · Turn a call into usable notes

Here are my rough notes from a client call. Pull out: what they said in their own words, what they actually need as opposed to what they asked for, their real constraint, and the one thing that would make them decide.

Mark clearly anything you are inferring rather than reading. Do not fill gaps.

NOTES: [paste]

The "just looking" lead

Most agents drop them. They are the best long-term pipeline you have, because nobody else is still there in four months. The Day 30 and Day 60 emails from Module 3 exist entirely for this person — both give something useful and ask for nothing.

The rule that keeps this honest

Every draft in this course is a draft. You read it, you fix the line that is not true, and you send it. An email that goes out with an invented detail costs more than every hour this course saves.

Module 10

Listing Visuals

30 minutes

A listing with a short video gets watched. Hiring a videographer for a $300k listing rarely pays. This module covers the middle option.

What AI visuals can and cannot do here

Be clear on the line, because crossing it is a real problem in this industry:

FineNot fine
A short branded intro or outro for your listing videoAltering photos of the actual property
Neighbourhood b-roll and establishing shotsRemoving or adding anything inside a room
Social graphics, market update visuals, your own headshot workAnything that changes what a buyer would see in person
Clearly labelled virtual staging of an empty roomUnlabelled staging presented as the real thing

The rule underneath all of it: a buyer should never be surprised at the door. Enhancement of your marketing is fine. Misrepresentation of the property is a licence problem, and no time saved is worth that.

The workflow

Tools like Higgsfield generate short video and images from a written description. The quality of what you get is decided almost entirely by how specifically you describe the shot — the same lesson as every other module in this course.

Prompt 45 · Write the shot description

[Project: Listings]

I need a 6-second establishing clip to open a listing video. Write the generation prompt for it. Describe: time of day, weather, camera movement, what is in frame, and the mood. Be specific enough that two different tools would produce something similar.

Do not describe the property itself — this is neighbourhood context only, and I will use real footage for the home.

NEIGHBOURHOOD: [describe it]

Prompt 46 · The 20-second script

[Project: Listings]

Write a 20-second script to be spoken over listing footage. First three seconds must work as a standalone hook. One fact per sentence. Last line is a concrete next step. No superlatives.

LISTING: [paste your description from Module 2]

Prompt 47 · Disclosure line

Write a one-line disclosure I can put on a virtually staged image, in plain language a buyer understands immediately. It must be honest and not look like fine print.

Label it, always

Any image that is generated or virtually staged carries a visible label. Not in the caption, not in the last slide — on the image. It costs nothing and it is the single thing that keeps AI visuals from becoming a complaint.

Module 11

The Weekly Hour

60 minutes, once a week

Everything above collapses into one hour on Monday morning. This is the module that makes the rest stick.

The part everyone skips

The last ten minutes. Every week, paste back into your voice file the one thing the AI wrote that you edited most heavily, along with your edit. Within a month the profile stops producing writing you have to fix, because it has learned the corrections you keep making.

What this course does not do

It does not get you leads, and no prompt will. It takes the writing out of your week so the hours go to the two things that actually close business: talking to people and being in front of property. If you use the time you save to do more admin, you have gained nothing.
